Janka Kupała · 1906-1907

Viecier, i sokał, i ja…

I viecier, i sokał, i ja —
Syny adnoj doli-žyccia,
Navodzim markotnyja my
I dumy, i piesni adny.

Razniaŭ viecier krylli svaje,
Lacić, i šumić, i piaje;
Piaje a svabodzie svajej,
Piaje a niavoli ludziej.

Jak viecier, i ja b tak lacieŭ,
Jak viecier, ab tym samym pieŭ,
Jak viecier, svabodny moj duch,
Choć ruki zakuty ŭ łancuh.

Pad zorami sokał zavis,
Hladzić pa-arlinamu ŭniz.
Tam sokału svietu nie žal;
Milej jamu niebnaja dal.

Jak sokał, i ja b mknuŭ da zor:
Tam sonca, razdolle, prastor;
Biez žalu b pakinuŭ ziamlu,
I chatku, i dolu svaju.

I viecier, i sokał, i ja —
Syny adnoj doli-žyccia;
Navodzim markotnyja my
I dumy, i piesni adny.

Modern łacinka transcription. Source: Belarusian Palichka, knihi.com; collected works, vol. 1 (1995). [12]

01 · Origins

Who invented it?

No single established inventor can be named. Belarusian łacinka developed as a tradition of writing Belarusian in Latin letters, rather than as one person's invention on one particular date. [1, 2]

Early surviving examples are associated with the late sixteenth century. In the seventeenth century, Belarusian dialogue was recorded in Latin script, including theatrical interludes. Polish graphic conventions strongly influenced early practice. [1]

During the nineteenth century, łacinka became an important vehicle for modern Belarusian literature. Letters and spelling conventions continued to change; today's chart cannot simply be imposed on every old edition. [1, 2]

Ask who wrote, printed, circulated and codified it.

02 · The nineteenth century

Writers and books

Vincient Dunin-Marcinkievič: his Belarusian translation of the first two books of Pan Tadeusz appeared in Vilnius in 1859 in Latin script and was confiscated. This was the Belarusian translation, not Mickiewicz's Polish original. [1, 6]

Francišak Bahuševič: Dudka biełaruskaja appeared in Kraków in 1891 under the name Maciej Buračok. Smyk biełaruski followed in 1894 under Symon Reŭka z-pad Barysava. Both were printed in łacinka. [5]

Kastuś Kalinoŭski: editor and one of the authors of Mužyckaja praŭda (1862-1863), a newspaper printed in Latin script. His role here is chiefly that of publicist and editor. [7]

An author, an editor and a codifier play different roles.

03 · The early twentieth century

Readers and reformers

Ciotka (Ałaiza Paškievič): the publisher's advertisement in Kupala's Žalejka (1908) lists her primer alongside a Cyrillic edition. Its title in a modern łacinka reading is Pieršaje čytańnie dla dzietak biełarusaŭ; this is not a diplomatic transcription of the advertisement. [6]

Janka Kupała: Huślar (1910) is a concrete example of his poetry in Latin script. This does not make every edition of his work Latin-script: the inspected Žalejka (1908) has a Cyrillic main text. [6, 8]

Branisłaŭ Taraškievič: his school grammar appeared in Vilnius in 1918 in both scripts. He codified an existing language and writing tradition; he did not invent łacinka from nothing. [4]

A printed Latin edition is not automatically evidence of a Latin-script autograph.

07 · Combinations

Letters working together

CombinationExample
ch = ххата / chata
dz = дздзе / dzie
dź = дзьмядзьведзь / miadźviedź
dž = джджэм / džem
šč = шчшчупак / ščupak

Ch, dz and dž are stable combinations; šč represents a sequence of š + č. Dź marks softness. [2, 3]

Мядзьведзь is the classical spelling. Preserving modern мядзведзь gives miadzviedź instead. Keep the two source spellings distinct.

Read chata, džem and ščupak aloud; cover the examples and reconstruct their Cyrillic forms.

Caron: č, š, ž. Acute: ć, ń, ś, ź.
Breve: ŭ.

08 · A sound without a syllable

The cover letter: Ŭ

CyrillicŁacinka
БеларусьBiełaruś
беларусаўbiełarusaŭ
словаsłova
слоўsłoŭ
быўbyŭ
воўкvoŭk

Latin Ŭ ŭ corresponds to Belarusian Ў ў. Its mark is a breve: a small curve, not a straight bar. The base letter is U, not Y. [2, 3]

Ў does not form a separate syllable. English w offers a rough starting point; listening to Belarusian speech is better. [3]

У / u and ў / ŭ are different.
Notice the letter in a complete word.

09 · Hard and soft

Ł and L

Hard л: łSoft л: l
лапа / łapaлямпа / lampa
лук / łukлюк / luk
школа / škołaшкольны / školny
бусел / busiełсоль / sol

The last consonant in busieł is hard; in sol it is soft. Before i, soft л is written l. [2, 3]

After soft l, use a, e, o, u for я, е, ё, ю: ля / la, ле / le, лё / lo, лю / lu. Do not add an extra i mechanically.

If you know Polish, take special care: Belarusian ł here represents hard л, not the usual modern Polish pronunciation of ł.

лес / les · лёд / lod · людзі / ludzi

10 · Ć Ń Ś Ź

Softness: acute or i?

CyrillicŁacinka
сольsol
коньkoń
сябарsiabar
ціхаcicha
восеньvosień
піцьpić

At a word's end, an acute can mark softness: дзень / dzień, конь / koń, пісаць / pisać. There is no separate letter for ь. [2, 3]

Before a vowel, i usually does the work: ня / nia, ця / cia, ся / sia, зя / zia. Compare дзень / dzień with дня / dnia.

Do not add both an acute and i automatically. Check the consonant's position and the source spelling.

L is already soft: соль / sol.

11 · Я Е Ё Ю

Choosing j or i

PositionWriting
At the startя / ja · е / je · ё / jo · ю / ju
After a vowelмая / maja · знаю / znaju
After a consonantня / nia · не / nie · нё / nio · ню / niu
After soft lля / la · ле / le · лё / lo · лю / lu

A separate й before a vowel is written j. The apostrophe is therefore not copied mechanically: сям’я / siamja, аб’ява / abjava, надвор’е / nadvorje. [3]

Find the vowel, examine what precedes it, then check separately for l and for a distinct j sound.

яма / jama · мая / maja
няма / niama · лямпа / lampa

12 · Orthography

One script, several choices

Modern source / renderingClassical source / rendering
снег / sniehсьнег / śnieh
свет / svietсьвет / śviet
песня / piesniaпесьня / pieśnia

Classical orthography marks assimilative softness in some positions where modern spelling does not. The difference may already exist in the Cyrillic source. [3, 4]

Choose a main approach for your notebook and label other spellings when quoting them.

Traditional łacinka is not identical to geographical romanization systems: its distinction between ł and l is one useful sign. [2, 9]

Choose the source spelling first.

14 · A first reading

A stork by the house

Раніца. Каля хаты стаіць дрэва. На дрэве гняздо. У гняздзе бусел. Я гляджу на птушку і пішу ў кнізе: «Добры дзень!»

Ranica. Kala chaty staić dreva. Na drevie hniazdo. U hniazdzie busieł. Ja hladžu na ptušku i pišu ŭ knizie: «Dobry dzień!»

Morning. A tree stands by the house. There is a nest in the tree and a stork in the nest. I look at the bird and write in the book: 'Good day!'

Original practice text, not a literary quotation. Find ch, dz, dž, soft l, ie and ŭ. Copy three words you recognized without the Cyrillic.

Why kala, but słova?
The л in каля is soft; in слова it is hard.

Try it yourself

4. Choose: яма - jama or iama? Няма - njama or niama? Мая - maja or maia?

18 · Your own page

Letters by hand

Write slowly: Ŭ ŭ · Ł ł · Ć ć · Ń ń · Ś ś · Ź ź · Č č · Š š · Ž ž.

Keep the curved breve, slanted acute and angled caron distinct. Do not let the stroke of ł disappear into a ruled line.

Then write busieł, hniazdo and Biełaruś. Add a date and a sentence of your own.

20 · Janka Kupała

Wind, falcon, and I

Вецер, і сокал, і я… / Viecier, i sokał, i ja…

This is the closest match to the book's theme: flight embodies a spirit that remains free despite constraint. The following excerpt follows the Cyrillic text in Kupala's collected works, volume 1 (1995), as presented by Belarusian Palichka. [12]

Як вецер, свабодны мой дух,
Хоць рукі закуты ў ланцуг.

Jak viecier, svabodny moj duch,
Choć ruki zakuty ŭ łancuh.

English sense: My spirit is free as the wind, though my hands are chained.

The łacinka here is our modern reading transcription, not a facsimile of an early edition. Other editions have a different second line; we do not mix them.

v ie c ie r · ŭ · ł
Notice ie, short ŭ and hard ł.

21 · Janka Kupała

A prayer to the wind

Мая малітва / Maja malitva

Choose the poem beginning «Ва ўсяку мінуту…». Kupala wrote more than one poem with this title. This one addresses sun, wind, water, sky and the wider world. [13]

Малюся свабоднаму ветру—віхуры,
Што лётае птушкай ад краю да краю,

Malusia svabodnamu vietru—vichury,
Što lotaje ptuškaj ad kraju da kraju,

English sense: I pray to the free whirlwind, flying like a bird from one edge of the world to the other.

The Cyrillic excerpt follows the 1926 collected-edition text on Wikisource. The łacinka transcription and English sense are prepared for this reader; neither is presented as an original historical translation.

лётае / lotaje · птушкай / ptuškaj
After soft l, ё becomes o.

22 · Jakub Kołas

Freedom and open space

Сымон-музыка / Symon-muzyka

A longer narrative poem about a gifted musician. It offers a further reading path for the artist's longing for freedom and room to create. [14]

This edition provides a reading recommendation only. No passage from Kołas's poem, Latin-script transcription of a passage, or English translation of a passage is reproduced here.

Read the work in an available edition and record its title, publisher and date. Notice who is speaking, and how music changes the meaning of freedom in the scene.

In your own words, answer: What can art express that ordinary speech cannot? Does the musician seek escape, belonging, or both?

These are original discussion prompts, not quotations or translations. The linked source is in Cyrillic; this recommendation does not imply that it was originally printed in Latin letters.

Read first. Then describe the scene in your own words.
